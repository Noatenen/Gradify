ROUND 2 · SUBMISSION WORKFLOWS VS. TEAM WORK, AND THE 5-DAY RE-ENTRY TEST

Not all "tasks" are the same kind of work.

The previous round assumed one undifferentiated task list. Looking closer, Motiva actually holds two different workflows wearing the same word "task": a Submission (prepare → submit → wait on the supervisor → revise or get approved — a linear approval workflow with a gatekeeper) and a Team Task (plan → work → done — ordinary collaborative work, no approval gate). Forcing both into one visual model either strips the workflow feeling from submissions or adds fake "stages" to team work that never had them. They should share materials and rhythm, but not necessarily the same row shape.

Every concept below is also stress-tested against the hardest real scenario: a student back after five days away, needing to regain context in 30 seconds — what changed, what needs them, what can wait, where the project stands, what to continue.

2aTwo Lanes, One Rhythm

Submissions as a workflow strip; team tasks as milestone rows. Same type, spacing, materials.

הגשות — זרימת אישור

משימות צוות — אבן דרך נוכחית

HIERARCHY

  1. Submissions zone — each a mini stage-strip (prepare/submitted/review/decision)
  2. Team tasks zone — milestone-grouped rows, Momentum Mode tags

Rationale: a submission genuinely has stages and a gatekeeper — visualizing that is honest, not decorative. Team work doesn't, so it stays as plain rows.

+ Each type gets the model it actually deserves; reuses the Dashboard's stage-node language for submissions, so it feels instantly familiar.

– Two zones means two places to check — needs a strong recap layer on top (see 2c) or students may miss one.

Scalability: handles growth well — each zone scrolls independently; a milestone with 20 team tasks doesn't crowd the submissions view.

HIERARCHY

  1. Milestone groups, as before
  2. Every row identical in shape; a small trailing label carries the type + state

Rationale: optimizes purely for "one list to scan," at the cost of the workflow feeling submissions deserve.

+ Simplest to build and scan; nothing feels "extra."

– A submission stuck "waiting on the supervisor" reads exactly like a team task someone forgot about — the meaningful difference (it's not stalled, it's out of the student's hands) gets lost.

Scalability: fine at low volume; a long single list gets harder to scan as both task types grow together.

2cRecap-First Workspace

Built around the 5-days-away test: "what changed" leads everything.

מאז שהיית כאן לאחרונה

הגשות

משימות צוות · אבן דרך נוכחית

HIERARCHY

  1. Compact "what changed" recap (dismissible, reuses the Dashboard's notification-row language)
  2. Submissions zone
  3. Team tasks zone, milestone-grouped

Rationale: treats "regaining context" as its own problem, not something the organization scheme should have to solve implicitly.

+ Directly passes the 30-second re-entry test; also reassures on a normal daily visit ("nothing changed" is itself calming).

– Needs care so the recap doesn't become a second inbox competing with the real Notifications feed.

Scalability: recap should hard-cap at 2–3 items regardless of absence length, or it starts to feel like an unread-mail pile.

הגשות — נשארות כרשימת זרימה נפרדת

HIERARCHY

  1. Three-column awareness board for team tasks
  2. Submissions kept separate, as a workflow list

Rationale: included because the brief explicitly allows it — but a board implies parallel work-in-progress management, which doesn't match a solo student's actual task volume (a handful of items, not a team backlog).

+ Genuinely fast "how much is left" read at a glance.

– Columns read as a management tool, not a companion — the single pattern most likely to make Motiva feel like Trello. Recommended against.

Scalability: ironically weakest at low volume — three columns with 1–2 cards each look sparse and administrative rather than calm.

RECOMMENDATION

2c's recap band, on top of 2a's two-lane split. Submissions get the stage-strip they've earned as a real approval workflow; team tasks stay milestone-grouped rows carrying the Momentum Mode tags from round 1. A capped, dismissible "what changed" band sits above both, so the 5-day re-entry case is handled explicitly rather than hoping the structure implies it.

Explicitly not recommending 2d's Kanban — it's the one pattern here that reads as a management tool rather than a companion, exactly what the brief asks Motiva to avoid becoming.

TASKS · UX & INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE EXPLORATION

Not a checklist. A way to decide what's next.

The Dashboard answers "where am I." Tasks has to answer "how do I move forward?" — not "here is all your data." Four IA concepts below, low-fidelity on purpose: the question at this stage is structure, not finish. All four assume the same Design System (materials, color, type, motion) already established.

A final-year student opening this page is usually asking one of: what should I work on first, what's blocking me, what belongs to which milestone, what can I clear quickly, what's overdue, what needs someone else. Each concept below prioritizes a different one of those.

AMilestone Workspace

Tasks grouped by milestone — the project's shape leads.

אבן דרך 2 · פיתוח אב־טיפוס (נוכחית)

אבן דרך 1 · מחקר ואפיון — הושלמה

אבן דרך 3 · הערכת משתמשים — נעולה

HIERARCHY

  1. Current milestone, tasks expanded
  2. Past milestones, collapsed to a summary line
  3. Future milestones, collapsed and visually locked

Rationale: mirrors the Dashboard timeline directly — a student who just saw "milestone 2, 68%" lands on a page structured the exact same way.

+ Strongest continuity with the Dashboard; always shows progression, not just a pile of work.

– A single blocking task can hide inside a milestone group instead of surfacing immediately.

BPriority Workspace

One ranked list — urgency leads, milestone is context.

דורש טיפול עכשיו

הבא בתור

בהמתנה / חוסם על מישהו אחר

HIERARCHY

  1. Overdue / blocking, always on top
  2. Next in line, ranked by deadline + effort
  3. Waiting on someone else, deprioritized visually

Rationale: answers "what should I work on first" with zero interpretation required — the order is the answer.

+ Fastest decision path; nothing to scan past.

– Milestone progress disappears into a tag — the "how far along am I" feeling the Dashboard just built gets lost.

CFocus Workspace

Only current work is visible. Everything else stays out of sight.

עכשיו

3 הושלמו · מוסתר · 5 עתידי · מוסתר

HIERARCHY

  1. Only what's actionable today
  2. Completed / future collapsed behind a single quiet toggle

Rationale: the calmest possible surface — nothing to feel behind on, because nothing irrelevant is visible.

+ Lowest anxiety, lowest visual load of all four.

– Planning ahead requires an extra click; can feel opaque to students who want to see the whole runway.

DProject Journey

Tasks live as steps on the path itself, not a separate list.

משימות השלב הנוכחי מוצגות מתחת לצומת שלו

HIERARCHY

  1. The path itself, horizontal or vertical
  2. Tasks nested directly under their step

Rationale: makes the metaphor literal — "your tasks are the next steps of the journey," not a database view of them.

+ Most emotionally resonant; progress and work are the same visual object.

– Weakest at handling many tasks per step or fast scanning — the path can get visually heavy fast.

THE UNIQUELY-MOTIVA IDEA

Momentum Mode — match the task to the energy you actually have

Traditional task tools sort by due date or priority — useful for a sprint, wrong for a six-month thesis. The real failure mode for final-project students isn't forgetting a task, it's opening the list between classes, seeing ten equally-heavy items, and closing it again. That's where procrastination actually starts.

Every task carries one lightweight tag for the kind of effort it needs — not a due date, not a priority level: Quick win (under 15 minutes, no setup), Deep work (needs a real focus block), or Waiting on someone (not yours to push right now). A single filter row lets a student ask "what can I actually do with the 20 minutes I have right now" — turning the task list from a wall of obligation into something that fits the moment they're in.

⚡ ניצחון מהיר ◐ עבודה מרוכזת ⋯ ממתין לאחר

RECOMMENDATION

A, focus-collapsed like C, filtered by Momentum Mode. Milestone grouping keeps the direct visual handoff from the Dashboard timeline — the student already knows "milestone 2" is where they live; Tasks should open on exactly that group, expanded, with past milestones collapsed to a single line and future ones visually locked (borrowing Focus Workspace's discipline about hiding irrelevant time). Momentum Mode sits as a quiet filter row at the top of the current milestone's tasks.

Priority Workspace (B) is worth keeping as an alternate view/toggle for students who want a flat urgency list, but shouldn't be the default — it would sever the Dashboard's progress framing. Project Journey (D) is the most beautiful idea but risks becoming unreadable once a milestone has more than 4–5 tasks; better saved for a milestone-detail or celebratory view than the everyday Tasks page.
